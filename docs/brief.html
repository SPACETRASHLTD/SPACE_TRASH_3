The Problem

You're a calendar app, not an agent.

Right now your week is half-spent reconciling artist availability across Google, Apple, and Overture — chasing artists to re-paste their calendar key, eyeballing five tabs to find who's free June 14, then negotiating offers one phone call at a time.

Pain 1

Artists never keep Overture's calendar field current

Every time their schedule changes, someone has to remind them to re-paste their calendar URL. It's a permanent low-grade headache.

Pain 2

Finding "who's free Saturday" means opening every artist's calendar

For one date. Across however many artists. Then doing it again for the next date.

Pain 3

Offers go out one phone call at a time

No way to send "first to accept wins" to three artists at once. So you walk down the list, hoping the first call says yes.

The Solution

One privacy-preserving sync.
One dashboard. One batch SMS.

Each artist clicks a link once and grants us their Google Calendar — but in a mode where Google itself only tells us when they're busy, not what they're doing. We pipe those opaque "Busy" blocks straight into Overture so the artist record there is always current with zero effort from anyone.

Then you get a master calendar: click a day, see who's free, pick three of them, fire off SMS offers with accept/decline links and a 24-hour clock. First yes wins; the others get released automatically.

How it works

Four steps. One of them is "wait".

1

Artist clicks once

They tap a link, sign into Google, grant FreeBusy-only access. ~30 seconds.

2

You paste a URL into Overture

One iCal feed URL per artist into Overture's "Internet Calendars" field. Once. Forever.

3

Polls every 15 minutes

Their busy blocks flow into Overture without anyone touching anything ever again.

4

You compose offers

Pick a date, pick artists, hit send. SMS fans out, accept/decline links, 24-hour clock.

Privacy

The artist's calendar stays the artist's calendar.

We use Google's FreeBusy API, not the events API. Google itself returns nothing more than time ranges — no titles, no locations, no attendees, regardless of how much access the artist has granted. This is the strongest privacy boundary Google offers, and it's enforced on their server, not promised by ours.

What we see vs. what we don't

What we see
  • "Busy from 3:00 PM to 5:00 PM on May 19"
  • That's it.
What we don't see
  • Event titles ("Dentist", "Date night")
  • Locations, descriptions, attendees
  • Notes, attachments, recurrence rules
  • Anything but the time range

Offboarding is one click: revoke the artist's feed token, the .ics URL we publish 404s, and Overture's import goes blank automatically.
